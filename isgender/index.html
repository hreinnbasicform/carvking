Iceland · public finance · register

The offices that score sex in the budget.

Gendered finance in Iceland is not one agency. It is a Finance Ministry procedure, a Justice Ministry watchdog, a complaints panel, a small fund, a city hall, and a bond sold to one buyer.

1 October 2026IS / genderAwards and human rights

Iceland has practised gender budgeting since 2009. Since 2016 it has been mandatory at state level under the Public Finance Act. The pen sits in the Ministry of Finance and Economic Affairs. The equality agency does not write the budget. It sits on the project board, runs equal-pay administration, and reports to the Minister of Justice.

This page lists governmental and public entities that evaluate, tag, or adjudicate gendered finance. It does not list every ministry that has an equality plan. A plan is not an evaluation.

The register

Ministry of Finance and Economic Affairs

Holds the pen

Fjármála- og efnahagsráðuneytið

Lead office for gender budgeting. Practice from 2009; mandatory at state level from 2016. Draws on Council of Europe and OECD guidance. Frames the work in a five-year plan — the published plan covered 2019–2023 — and updates a base report analysing expenditure areas by sex. Line ministries account for the gender impact of budget proposals. The overall gender impact is written into the budget bill, and again into supplementary budget bills. Progress is reported in each minister’s annual report. A working group on sex-disaggregated statistics sits under this machinery.

Steering committee on gender budgeting

Scores the budget

Stýrihópur / verkefnisstjórn kynjaðrar fjárlagagerðar

Led by Finance. Every ministry sits on it. The Directorate of Equality has a seat. This is the actual evaluation body. It is a committee, not an institution with its own budget line.

Line ministries

Produce the assessments

Ráðuneytin

Gender objectives are written into the five-year fiscal plan and followed in the budget bill. A 2025 supplementary bill noted that extra road-maintenance spending creates jobs held mostly by men during construction, and treated that as a limited long-run equality effect.

Alþingi, Budget Committee

Receives, does not write

Alþingi · fjárlaganefnd

Parliament is the oversight node. The gender-impact chapter travels with the budget bill. The committee does not run its own gender-budget unit.

Ministry of Justice

Owns the equality portfolio

Dómsmálaráðuneytið

Equality sat in the Prime Minister’s Office from the start of 2019. From 1 March 2025 gender-equality matters belong to the Ministry of Justice: the Gender Equality Act, the Administration of Equality Act, the equal-treatment acts, the Gender Autonomy Act, the Directorate of Equality, the complaints committee, and the Equality Fund. Justice does not lead gender budgeting.

Directorate of Equality

Scores pay, monitors law

Jafnréttisstofa · under the Minister of Justice

Special institution under Act no. 151/2020. Director: Martha Lilja Olsen. Sits on the gender-budgeting project board. From 1 September 2026, equal-pay certification is replaced by a triennial pay-gap report for employers of 50 or more. Employers with 25 or more still file an equality plan.

Equality Complaints Committee

Adjudicates

Kærunefnd jafnréttismála

Quasi-judicial panel under the Justice portfolio. Rules on complaints under the equality statutes, including pay. It does not tag the state budget.

Icelandic Equality Fund

Spends, does not score

Jafnréttissjóður Íslands

Founded 19 June 2015. Finances projects and research. A grant fund is not an evaluation office. See the ten-year ledger.

Statistics Iceland

Supplies the numbers

Hagstofa Íslands

Sex-disaggregated statistics are the prerequisite the Finance Ministry names for every gender analysis. It does not issue a gender verdict on the budget.

City of Reykjavík, Human Rights Office

Municipal scorer

Reykjavíkurborg · Mannréttindaskrifstofa · KFS

Formal gender budgeting since 2011. Analyses services and runs equality-impact screening on new projects, changes to the financial plan, and investment-plan items.

University of Iceland

Public institution, partial

Háskóli Íslands

After the state and the city, the university ran its own gender-budgeting programme. Not a national evaluator.

Human rights offices

Icelandic Human Rights Institution

New, parliamentary

Mannréttindastofnun Íslands · Act no. 88/2024 · opened 1 May 2025

Independent national human rights institution under Alþingi, Paris Principles. Monitoring, advice, research, education, help to the public, and rights protection for disabled people. Too new for a ten-year money series.

Ministry of Justice, human rights desk

Portfolio, not a fund

Dómsmálaráðuneytið · mannréttindi og jafnrétti

Same minister holds equality and human rights. Treaty list, European Court of Human Rights file, Universal Periodic Review. Does not score the budget.

Ministry for Foreign Affairs

Pays outside bodies

Utanríkisráðuneytið

OHCHR core support 20 m.kr. a year for 2021–2023, 60 m.kr. total. ISHR agreement 20 m.kr., signed 2 September 2025. Other-grants line to the NGO Mannréttindaskrifstofa Íslands, 31.3 m.kr. over 2017–2024. That centre is not a state agency.

Reykjavík Human Rights Office and Council

City scorer and payer

Mannréttindaskrifstofa · Mannréttindaráð

Net spend 302 m.kr. in 2024 and 250 m.kr. in 2025. Awards the annual prize. The accessibility recognition is a diploma, not cash.

The law, short

  • Public Finance Act no. 123/2015 — gender budgeting mandatory at state level from 2016.
  • Act on Equal Status and Equal Rights Irrespective of Gender no. 150/2020.
  • Act on the Administration of Matters Concerning Equality no. 151/2020.
  • Act on Gender Autonomy no. 80/2019. Equal-treatment acts no. 85/2018 and 86/2018.
  • Icelandic Human Rights Institution, Act no. 88/2024, opened 1 May 2025.
  • Equal-pay reporting regime, in force 1 September 2026.
